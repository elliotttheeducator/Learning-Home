Naming an angle

Two arms meet at the vertex. Name the angle with three letters, the vertex in the middle:  ∠ABC or ∠CBA.
Its size is written with a lower-case letter: a°.

Types of angle

Acutebetween 0° and 90°
Rightexactly 90°
Obtusebetween 90° and 180°
Straightexactly 180°
Reflexbetween 180° and 360°
Revolutionexactly 360°

More words

Collinearpoints on one straight line
Concurrent3 or more lines through one point
Parallelnever meet:  AB ∥ DC
Perpendicularmeet at 90°:  AB ⊥ CD
Watch out. A protractor has two scales. Put its centre on the vertex, line one arm up with 0°, and read the scale that starts at 0 on that arm. A reflex angle is 360° minus the angle on the other side.

7BAdjacent and vertically opposite angles
By the end of this lesson
  • I can use the angle sums 90°, 180° and 360° to find unknown angles
  • I can use vertically opposite angles
  • I can write the reason for every step, not just the number
Key ideasfill the gaps as we go
Adjacent angles sit side by side. They share a vertex and an arm. Here ∠AOB and ∠BOC are adjacent.

Four facts that find a missing angle

Complementaryin a right angle, they add to 90°
a + b = 90
Supplementaryon a straight line, they add to 180°
a + b = 180
Revolutionall the way round, they add to 360°
a + b + c = 360
Vertically oppositeacross a crossing, they are equal
Perpendicular lines meet at 90°. We write AB ⊥ CD.
30° is the complement of 60° (they make 90°). 130° is the supplement of 50° (they make 180°).
Watch out. Diagrams may not be drawn to scale, so calculate, never measure. Every answer gets its reason, written in words: “angles on a straight line add to 180°”.
